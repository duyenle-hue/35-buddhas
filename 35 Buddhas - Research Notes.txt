# 35 Buddhas — Research Notes

Oct 21, 2026 · Team Resources D05

## What these are

Your 35 photos are the 35 Confession Buddhas (Tam Thập Ngũ Phật; Sanskrit: the Buddhas of the Triskandhaka or "Three Heaps" sutra). They are recited and bowed to in one of Mahāyāna Buddhism's best-known purification practices: you name each Buddha, take refuge, and confess and let go of harmful actions. Shakyamuni (Thích Ca) is #1 and leads the other 34. The list is the same in Vietnamese, Chinese and Tibetan practice; only the art and ritual around it differ.

The set is complete: 35 of 35, numbered in the standard order. There is no traditional 36th Buddha in this list (see "Things to fix or decide").

## Where they come from

The names come from the Upāli chapter (Ưu Ba Ly Hội) of the Mahāratnakūṭa (Kinh Đại Bảo Tích, Taishō T310, ch. 24), also translated on its own as the Quyết Định Tỳ Ni Kinh (T325). In it, the Buddha teaches that a bodhisattva who has broken precepts should recite these 35 names day and night to purify the fault.

- Chinese/Vietnamese line: the Kinh Quán Hư Không Tạng Bồ Tát (T409) tells practitioners to bow to the Buddhas of the ten directions and recite the 35 names, and itself notes the names come from the Upāli chapter. The 35 later became the second half of the Vietnamese Hồng Danh Sám Hối liturgy.
- Tibetan line: the same text is called the Sūtra of the Three Heaps (confession, rejoicing, dedication). Nāgārjuna and later Tsongkhapa wrote visualization manuals, which is where the colors, hand gestures and objects in your paintings come from.
- None of the Chinese sutras I checked give each Buddha a direction or a named world. That layer comes from the Tibetan visualization (next section).

Names vary slightly by translation. One example: #35 is "Bảo Liên Hoa Thiện Trụ Ta La Thọ Vương" (sāla-tree king) in the Chinese chant but "Tu Di Sơn Vương" (Mount Meru king) in your set. Both render the same Sanskrit, Ratnapadma-supratiṣṭhita-śailendrarāja. Śailendra means "lord of mountains", so your set's version is closer to the Sanskrit.

## How your photo set is organized

Your images are Tibetan-style paintings with Vietnamese captions, and each card has three layers a website can use.

1. Title — "Nam Mô [direction] [world] Thế Giới [name] Phật". The direction and world (in italics on the card) place each Buddha in a pure land around Shakyamuni.
2. The painting — a distinct body color, hand gesture, and often a held object (sword, banner, tree, lotus, jewel, a stupa, a jeweled mountain on the head for #35). This follows the Nāgārjuna-style system in which Buddhas hold objects. Below the throne sit the five sense offerings: mirror (sight), lute (sound), conch of perfume (smell), fruit (taste) and silk (touch).
3. Caption — "Quy y Ngài, con diệt được…": what bowing to that Buddha is said to purify. These come from the Tibetan commentarial tradition, not from the sutra itself.

The directions follow a fixed cycle around Shakyamuni at the center: Above → E → SE → S → SW → W → NW → N → NE → Below. Three full rounds cover #2–#31; the last four take only the main points.

The captions sort into three kinds, useful for site filters: eons of negative karma (10 cards), harm from body, speech, mind or an emotion such as anger or pride (8), and a specific wrongdoing, from harming parents or the Sangha to slander or abandoning the Dharma (17).

## How people practice

- Vietnamese — Hồng Danh Sám Hối: temples chant 53 Buddha names (from Phổ Quang Phật), then these 35, then "Pháp Giới Tạng Thân A Di Đà Phật", bowing at each name. With the closing vows it comes to 108 prostrations, one for each of the 108 afflictions. Many temples hold it on the 14th and the last day of each lunar month.
- Tibetan — prostrations to the 35 Buddhas: practitioners visualize the 35 in the sky, recite each name with a full prostration, then the confession prayer. It is a standard daily or preliminary (ngöndro) practice, often done in sets of 100,000 prostrations.
- Shared idea: the four "powers" of purification — regret, refuge, resolve not to repeat, and a remedy (here, reciting the names). This is the clearest one-line explanation for a general audience.

## Things to fix or decide before the website

[ ] 35 or 36? The tradition has 35. If you want a 36th, the natural candidate is A Di Đà, who closes the Vietnamese chant — but label him as an addition, not part of the 35.
[ ] Card source: find out who published these cards, both for image credit and to confirm the Vietnamese world names.
[ ] Filename typos (the cards themselves are correct): #13 "Thấ Giới" → "Thế Giới"; #20 missing "Phương" after "Đông Bắc"; #29 "Hồng Diễm" → "Hồng Diệm" (紅焰); #4, #6, #9, #31 have lowercase words; folder name "35 Budhas" → "Buddhas".
[ ] Unicode: filenames are stored in decomposed form (NFD). Rename to plain ASCII slugs (e.g. 02-kim-cuong-bat-hoai.jpg) so web links don't break.
[ ] Framing the captions: "con diệt được… 10.000 kiếp" is devotional language. For a general audience, present it as "traditionally said to purify…".
[ ] Language: decide Vietnamese-first with English, or both equally; the table above already has both.
